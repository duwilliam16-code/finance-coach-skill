# Military, veteran and federal employee module

Read this when the person is active duty, Guard or Reserve, a veteran, a
military spouse, or a federal civilian employee. These groups have benefits and
traps that general advice misses.

**Same rule as everywhere: verify, don't recall.** This file tells you *what
to check*. It deliberately contains no dollar limits or rates - look those up
from the official source named, every time.

## Guard and Reserve, and active duty

- **SCRA (Servicemembers Civil Relief Act)** - protections for debts taken on
  *before* active service, including an interest-rate cap. Applies to
  obligations from before the service period, and usually must be **requested**
  with a copy of orders. Source: the statute (50 U.S.C. 3937) and the
  creditor's own SCRA page. Not automatic.
- **Credit card annual fee waivers** - several major issuers waive annual fees
  for servicemembers on active orders, sometimes for spouses too, under their
  **own policies** that go beyond the law. Terms differ by issuer and change.
  Tell them to check the issuer's military benefits page and request it with
  orders **while the orders are current**. Business cards may not qualify -
  ask the issuer.
- **Drill pay and orders change taxable income.** The Leave and Earnings
  Statement (LES) is the primary record. Use it, not a remembered monthly
  figure; bonuses and annual training can make military income far higher
  than "drill pay" suggests.
- **Combat zone pay** can be excluded from income. Source: IRS Publication 3.

## Veterans

- **VA disability compensation is not taxable income.** Never add it to
  adjusted gross income when estimating taxes or eligibility for anything
  income-based. Source: IRS Publication 525 and 3.
- VA disability rating can bring state benefits (property tax relief in many
  states). Point them to their state's veterans affairs office; rules vary.

## TSP (Thrift Savings Plan)

- The TSP is the federal and military 401(k). Source: tsp.gov.
- **The yearly employee contribution limit is per person, not per plan.** Someone
  with a TSP *and* a civilian 401(k) shares **one** limit across both. Each
  payroll only sees its own plan, so neither will stop an over-contribution -
  the person has to track it. Look up this year's limit at irs.gov.
- Matching depends on retirement system. Under BRS (Blended Retirement System)
  the government matches; under the legacy military system there is no match.
  Confirm which they are under.
- TSP funds have very low fees; the L (Lifecycle) funds are a reasonable
  one-fund default.

## Federal civilian employees

- **FERS** (Federal Employees Retirement System) has three parts: a pension, Social
  Security, and the TSP. Source: opm.gov.
- **Leaving federal service:** they can leave FERS contributions in for a
  future pension or take a refund. A refund usually forfeits that service
  credit. This is irreversible - explain it, then send them to OPM and a
  professional before deciding.
- **Military buy-back:** some federal employees can pay a deposit to count
  military service toward their FERS pension. Time-sensitive and worth asking
  HR about. Source: opm.gov.

## Military spouses

- Some issuer fee waivers extend to spouses - check each card.
- Moving with orders can affect state tax residency. Point them to the Military
  Spouses Residency Relief Act guidance and their state tax agency.

## Where to send them for free help

- **Personal Financial Managers / counselors** on base and through Military
  OneSource (militaryonesource.mil) - free and not selling anything.
- **VITA / MilTax** - free tax preparation for servicemembers through Military
  OneSource.
